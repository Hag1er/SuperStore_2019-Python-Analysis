The project

A retail company sells furniture, office supplies, and technology across the United States. It needs to know which products, places, and habits make profit, and which lose it.

The data

SheetContainsSize
OrdersProducts sold, dates, customers, places, sales, discount, profit9,994 rows
PeopleManager of each region4 rows
ReturnsOrders sent back800 rows

Time period: 3 Jan 2016 to 30 Dec 2019.

Questions I answered

  • Which category earns the most sales and profit?
  • Which sub-categories sell best, and which lose money?
  • Which regions and states perform best?
  • What affects profit the most?
  • Which category has the most returns?
  • Which shipping mode is fastest and most used?
  • How do sales change over time?

The workflow

Six steps from raw Excel file to dashboard. Choose a step to see what I did.

1. Data profiling: get to know the data

  • Loaded the three sheets with Python (pandas).
  • Checked the shape, data types, and unique values of every column.
  • Checked the dates: orders run from January 2016 to December 2019.
  • Checked missing values: only Postal Code had any, 11 rows (0.11%).
  • Checked correlations between Sales, Quantity, Discount, and Profit.
  • Found that the Returns sheet has 800 rows but only 296 different orders, so it has repeats.

Result: the data is in good shape and needs only small fixes.

2. Data cleaning: fix the problems

ProblemWhat I did
Column names with symbolsRenamed Sub-Category and Country/Region
Postal Code and Row ID were numbersChanged to text, because we never do math on them
11 missing postal codesAll were Burlington, Vermont. Filled with its main code, 05401
Outliers in Sales, Discount, ProfitChecked them. They are expensive items such as machines and phones, so they are real. Kept them
504 duplicate rows in ReturnsRemoved. 296 unique returned orders remain

3. Data manipulation: join the sheets

  • Joined Orders and People on Region, so each order shows its manager.
  • Joined the result with Returns on Order ID.
  • Orders with no match in Returns were marked Returned = No.

The final table keeps all 9,994 rows and adds the manager and the return status.

5. Exploratory analysis: ask questions

  • Used groupby and pivot tables for best products, cities, states, years, and months.
  • Compared category performance inside each region.

Key insights

Use the buttons on each chart to change what you see.

Category and region

Furniture sells a lot but keeps little. Its margin is 2.5%, against 17% for Technology and Office Supplies. Central is the weakest region at 7.9%.

Sales over time

Sales peak in September, November, and December. Those three months bring about 43% of all sales. 2019 was 56% higher than 2017.

Shipping speed and returns

Same Day is fastest (0.04 days). Standard Class is used most, in about 60% of order lines. 296 orders (5.9%) were returned, most of them Office Supplies.

More findings

Discounts hurt profit. Discount and Profit correlate at −0.22, while Discount and Sales barely relate (−0.03). Average discount is 15.6%, up to 80%.
Tables lose over $15,000 although they are a top-5 seller. The Cubify CubeX 3D Printer (double head) loses $8,880.
Best product: Canon imageCLASS 2200 Copier, $61,600 in sales and $25,200 profit.
Top states: California ($458K), New York ($311K), Texas ($170K). Top cities: New York City ($256K), Los Angeles ($176K), Seattle ($120K).
Central Furniture loses money (−$2,871), even though Central has more sales than the South.
